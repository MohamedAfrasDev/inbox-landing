# Landing page: content and structure

Oct 27, 2026 · @Afras

## 1. Page goal and pass bar

The page has one job: get a small-business owner who sells on Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp to join the waitlist. Every section either makes that owner feel understood or removes a reason to hesitate. Page sections, in order: problem, outcome, how it works, control, honest limits, one form.

- **Audience:** owners of small shops that sell through Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp in Sri Lanka, starting with product sellers (clothing, gifts, beauty, home goods).
- **One call to action:** "Join the waitlist", repeated at the top, middle and bottom. No second button such as "Watch demo" or "Contact sales".
- **Tone:** plain and direct, like a shop owner talking to another. No "AI-powered", "next-generation" or "revolutionise". Say chats, buyers, replies.
- **Pass bar:** at least 5% of targeted cold visitors join. Under 3% means the message is wrong. Count only real owners, tagged by source (UTM), never classmates.
- **Product name:** not decided. This document uses \[Product name\]; replace it everywhere at once.

**Proof you do not have yet.** The page has no testimonials, logos, customer counts or results. Leave them out. Use the real weaker forms: the founder's own story, a clearly labelled preview of the product, and a risk-free promise (free to join, no card). Add real proof only after real owners give it.

## 2. Page structure

Twelve sections, one scroll, each doing one job and handing off to the next. The slot column says which product screen goes in that section; the slots are defined in section 5.

| #   | Section               | Job                                                             | UI slot             |
| --- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| 1   | Nav bar               | Logo, three anchor links, one Join the waitlist button          | none                |
| 2   | Hero                  | Say what it is, who it is for and the outcome; show the product | UI-1 Dashboard      |
| 3   | Problem               | Make the owner feel seen: the lost enquiry                      | UI-2 Ad-leak scene  |
| 4   | Three jobs            | One inbox, who to answer first, replies in your voice           | UI-3, UI-4, UI-5    |
| 5   | While you are away    | Show the assistant answering and holding a customer             | UI-6 Customer phone |
| 6   | You stay in control   | Public and private fields, autopilot, test chat                 | UI-7 Knowledge base |
| 7   | See everything it did | The activity log as the trust screen                            | UI-8 Activity log   |
| 8   | Ad-leak story         | The boosted post that leaked leads                              | UI-9 Ad-leak report |
| 9   | How it works          | Three steps that feel achievable                                | UI-10 Setup         |
| 10  | What it will not do   | Honest limits, which builds trust                               | none                |
| 11  | FAQ                   | Answer the top objections                                       | none                |
| 12  | Waitlist and footer   | The one form, then a short footer                               | none                |

**Order logic.** Sections 2 to 3 name the pain. Sections 4 to 5 show the relief. Sections 6 to 7 earn trust, the biggest barrier to letting software talk to customers. Sections 8 to 11 handle doubts. Section 12 asks once.

**Section 9: How it works**

Heading: **Set it up in three steps.**

1. **Connect your apps.** Messenger and Instagram first. WhatsApp has extra steps, and we walk you through them.
2. **Add what customers ask about.** Upload your product list or paste a sheet. Mark which details are public and which are private.
3. **Test it, then switch it on.** Ask it questions yourself. When you are happy, turn on autopilot for after hours only.

Note under the steps: _Our target is about ten minutes for the first two steps. We will report the real time once owners try it._ \[UI-10: setup stepper\]

**Section 10: What it will not do**

Heading: **We keep this short on purpose.**

- It does not take orders or payments.
- It does not confirm stock or promise a discount. It hands those to you.
- It does not pretend to be you. Customers are told it is an AI assistant.
- It does not reply to everything. When it is unsure, it holds the customer and tells you.
- It cannot draft in your voice on day one. It needs your past chats first.

## 6. Waitlist form and tracking

A short form gets more sign-ups than a long one, and the answers still tell you who is serious. Use two steps: the email alone first, then three optional questions.

| Step | Field                                | Type                                                       | Why                                            |
| ---- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Email                                | Required                                                   | The only thing needed to join                  |
| 2    | What do you sell?                    | Dropdown: clothing, beauty, gifts, food, home goods, other | Picks the first business category to build for |
| 2    | Where do customers message you most? | Dropdown: Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp, about equal      | Tests the multi-channel assumption             |
| 2    | WhatsApp number for early access     | Optional                                                   | A way to reach owners for a short interview    |

**Tracking (set up before any traffic)**

- Store each sign-up with the UTM source, medium and campaign, the time, and whether step 2 was completed.
- Four events: page view, scroll to the form, step 1 submitted, step 2 submitted.
- Conversion = step 1 submissions divided by unique visitors from targeted traffic. Count real owners only; filter your own team and classmates by source tag.
- Block bots with a hidden field and a rate limit per address.
- Show one line of consent: "We use your email only for this waitlist."

**Where to send traffic.** Owners' groups on Facebook, a few boosted posts of your own aimed at small shops, and direct messages to shops you know. Each gets its own UTM tag, so you learn which source gave real owners.

**Follow-up within 48 hours.** Message the ten most serious sign-ups and ask for a 15-minute call. This gives the interview evidence your idea still lacks.

## 7. Self-critique and decisions before building

**Where this copy is weak**

- **The hero asks the visitor to believe a lot.** "Stop losing buyers" is a promise the product only partly keeps: it cannot reply on WhatsApp for every Sri Lankan number, and Meta limits automated replies to a 24-hour window. I softened the subhead (it no longer says "in your voice", since style learning is not ready on day one), but the headline is still strong. If a visitor feels misled later, the waitlist is full of people who will not convert.
- **"One inbox" is not a reason to join.** Meta Business Suite already offers a free combined inbox for Facebook and Instagram. The page must win on ranking, the ad-leak report and the Activity Log, which is why sections 4, 7 and 8 matter more than the layout.
- **No proof anywhere.** There are no real owners, results or quotes. The honest preview labels help, but a cold visitor has no reason to trust a student project. Add the first real quote after the first interviews and put it in the hero.
- **Three placeholders in the FAQ are unanswered.** Price, language support and data handling are the questions a careful owner asks first. A page with brackets cannot be published; decide each before launch.
- **Twelve sections is long.** If the scroll depth data shows most visitors leave before section 6, cut sections 7 to 9 into one block and move the form up.
- **The audience is a guess.** "Product sellers on Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp" is not yet backed by owners who told you they lose enquiries this way. Your own sighting of one boosted post is one case.

**Decide before building**

1. Product name and domain.
2. First category: product sellers (recommended) or another.
3. English only, or English plus Sinhala on the page and in version 1.
4. Price statement for the FAQ, even if it is "not decided".
5. Whether UI-2 (the problem scene) is drawn by you in the design canvas or by an illustration tool.
